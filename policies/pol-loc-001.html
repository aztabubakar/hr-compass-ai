Work Location, Interstate & International Work Policy

Explains why work location matters for payroll and legal compliance, lists Solara Dynamics' payroll-registered states and approved international EOR countries, and defines approval requirements for interstate and international work.

Purpose

Where an employee physically performs work determines which state or country's tax withholding, employment law, and benefits rules apply to that employee, independent of where the employee was originally hired or where their manager or team is based. This policy explains those requirements and the approval process for working outside an employee's assigned work location, whether the change is a short trip, an extended stay, or a permanent move.

Why Work Location Matters

Solara Dynamics must withhold and remit state payroll taxes, maintain workers' compensation coverage, and comply with employment law in every state where an employee performs work, even temporarily. Similar obligations exist at the country level for international work, including tax residency rules and, for the company itself, the risk of accidentally creating a taxable 'permanent establishment' in a country where Solara Dynamics has no registered legal entity.

Working from an unregistered state or an unsupported country can create unplanned tax filings, benefits complications, immigration risk, and legal exposure for both the company and the employee. For this reason, all interstate work beyond a short trip and all international work must be approved in advance through the process in Section 7, even when the Remote Work Policy's 'Work From Anywhere' allowance would otherwise seem to cover the duration.

Payroll-Registered States

Solara Dynamics is currently payroll-registered and able to employ staff in the following 14 states: Texas, Colorado, North Carolina, California, New York, Washington, Illinois, Georgia, Florida, Massachusetts, Virginia, Arizona, Oregon, and Tennessee. An employee's designated work location must be one of these states unless a documented exception has been approved by the People & Culture Team and Payroll. The current list of payroll-registered states is also published in Compass HR under 'Company Resources > Payroll States' and is updated whenever a new state registration is completed.

Temporary Interstate Work

Temporary work from a state other than an employee's designated work location is handled based on duration, as summarized below. These thresholds exist because most states only require additional withholding or registration once an employee has worked there for a meaningful period, but the exact threshold varies by state, so Solara Dynamics applies a single, simpler internal standard across all states for consistency:

  • Up to 30 consecutive calendar days in a state where Solara Dynamics is already payroll-registered: manager notice in Compass HR is sufficient; no additional approval is required.
  • 31 to 90 consecutive calendar days in an already-registered state: requires advance approval from Payroll and the People & Culture Team at least 15 business days before the work begins, to review state tax withholding obligations and confirm no additional registration is triggered.
  • More than 90 consecutive calendar days, or any duration in a state where Solara Dynamics is not payroll-registered: treated as a permanent relocation request (see Section 6), not temporary work, regardless of the employee's stated intent to eventually return to their original work location.

Permanent Relocation to a New State

Employees who wish to permanently relocate their designated work location to a new U.S. state must submit a relocation request at least 45 calendar days in advance through Compass HR. Relocation to an already payroll-registered state requires approval from the employee's VP and the People & Culture Team. Relocation to a state where Solara Dynamics is not yet registered additionally requires Payroll to confirm registration timelines, which typically take 30 to 60 calendar days, and may temporarily affect benefits enrollment continuity during the registration window.

Employees relocating permanently should also review the Paid Time Off Policy (POL-PTO-001), since certain state-specific rules (such as California's PTO payout requirements) apply based on the employee's new designated work location once the relocation is approved and effective.

International Remote Work

International remote work is permitted only in countries where Solara Dynamics maintains an active Employer of Record (EOR) agreement. As of this policy's effective date, the approved countries are the United Kingdom, Canada, Portugal, and Ireland. Employees whose permanent work location remains in the United States may request up to 6 consecutive weeks (30 business days) of temporary international remote work per calendar year in an approved country, sometimes referred to informally as a 'workation.' This 6-week allowance is measured in business days and does not reset separately for each trip; it is a per-employee, per-calendar-year total across all approved-country trips combined.

  • Requires Global Mobility review for tax residency and permanent-establishment risk before departure.
  • Requires a minimum of 20 business days' advance notice, submitted as a Work Location Change Request in Compass HR.
  • Requires the employee to complete and sign an International Remote Work Attestation confirming their travel dates and destination before departure.
  • Requires continued compliance with the Information Security & Data Protection Policy (POL-SEC-001), including restrictions on accessing Restricted-classification data from outside approved countries, and completion of any additional Data Handling Acknowledgment required for the employee's role.

Prohibited and Unsupported Locations

Any country not on the approved EOR list in Section 6 is not supported for remote work of any duration, including short trips combined with business travel, due to unresolved tax, immigration, and legal risk. This is true even if the employee holds citizenship or a right to work in that country, because the restriction is about Solara Dynamics' ability to lawfully employ someone performing work there, not about the individual employee's personal right to be in that country.

Exceptions require written approval from both the Chief Financial Officer and General Counsel and are granted only in unusual circumstances, such as a documented family emergency requiring a brief period of work from a non-approved country while the employee's primary residence and tax obligations remain unaffected. Such exceptions are rare, are evaluated case by case, and are never guaranteed.

Approval Process and Lead Time

All interstate work beyond 30 days and all international remote work requests are submitted through Compass HR as a 'Work Location Change Request,' which routes automatically to the employee's manager, Payroll, and the People & Culture Team (and additionally to Global Mobility for international requests). Approval status and any outstanding requirements are visible to the employee in Compass HR throughout the review, and the requester receives an email notification at each stage of approval or if additional information is needed.

Employees are encouraged to submit a Work Location Change Request as early as possible, even before all travel details are finalized, since the review process (particularly Global Mobility review for international requests) can take longer than the minimum lead times listed above during particularly busy periods.

Tax and Immigration Disclaimer

This policy describes Solara Dynamics' internal administrative process and does not constitute individual tax, immigration, or legal advice. Employees with questions about their personal tax residency, visa status, or right to work in a given location should consult a qualified professional in addition to following this policy. Approval of a Work Location Change Request confirms only that Solara Dynamics can support the arrangement administratively; it does not guarantee a particular personal tax outcome for the employee.

Worked Examples

Example (interstate, approvable with review): An employee whose designated work location is Austin, Texas asks to work from Denver, Colorado for six consecutive weeks (about 30 business days) to be near family. Colorado is a payroll-registered state and the duration falls in the 31-90 day tier, so the request is not automatically approved but can be approved with 15 business days' notice once Payroll and the People & Culture Team confirm there is no additional withholding issue.

Example (international, approvable with review): The same employee instead asks to work from Lisbon, Portugal for six consecutive weeks. Portugal is on the approved Employer of Record list and six weeks is exactly at the international allowance limit, so the request can be approved after Global Mobility review, 20 business days' notice, and a signed International Remote Work Attestation.

Example (not supported): The same employee asks to work from Thailand for six consecutive weeks. Thailand is not on the approved Employer of Record list, so the request is not supported under this policy regardless of duration, unless the CFO and General Counsel grant a rare written exception under Section 7.

Roles and Responsibilities

Payroll owns the list of payroll-registered states and is responsible for evaluating whether a temporary or permanent location change triggers new withholding obligations. Global Mobility owns the list of approved international EOR countries and leads tax-residency and permanent-establishment review for international requests. The People & Culture Team coordinates the overall Work Location Change Request workflow and is the primary point of contact for employees submitting a request.

Employees are responsible for submitting requests with accurate dates and destinations and for not beginning travel or work from a new location before their request is approved. Managers are responsible for confirming the request supports business continuity for the team before providing their approval.
