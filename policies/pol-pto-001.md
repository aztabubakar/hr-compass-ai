# Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy

**Document ID:** POL-PTO-001  
**Effective Date:** 2024-01-01  
**Version:** 2.1  
**Owner:** People & Culture Team

Describes how Solara Dynamics employees accrue, request, and use paid time off, including the Flexible PTO plan for Director-level and above employees.

## Purpose and Scope

Solara Dynamics, Inc. provides paid time off (PTO) so employees can rest, handle personal matters, travel, and return to work refreshed and productive. Time away from work is a normal and expected part of a healthy working life, and this policy is designed to make it simple to plan, request, and track. This policy applies to all U.S.-based full-time and part-time employees on Solara Dynamics' direct payroll. Contractors and employees engaged through an Employer of Record (EOR) in another country follow the leave terms in their contractor agreement or local EOR policy instead of this document, because those arrangements are already governed by local statutory leave requirements.

This policy should be read together with the Company Holiday Schedule Policy (POL-HOL-001), which governs company-observed holidays that do not count against PTO, and the Leave of Absence Policy (POL-LEV-001), which governs longer or specialized leaves such as parental or medical leave that are not part of routine PTO usage.

## Eligibility

All active full-time employees are eligible for PTO starting on their first day of employment, and accrual begins immediately even though usage in the first 30 days is limited as described below. Part-time employees scheduled for at least 20 hours per week are eligible for prorated PTO based on scheduled hours; part-time employees scheduled under 20 hours per week do not accrue PTO. Interns on a fixed-term unpaid or stipend basis, and employees on an approved unpaid personal leave of absence, do not accrue PTO during that period, though any PTO accrued and unused before the leave began remains available once the employee returns to active status.

New hires may request PTO as soon as it has accrued in Compass HR, but usage within the first 30 calendar days of employment requires manager approval and is discouraged except for pre-existing, unavoidable commitments made before the employee accepted their offer (for example, a wedding or a trip booked prior to the offer date). Managers should apply this 30-day guidance flexibly and in good faith rather than as a hard denial, since the intent is to encourage new hires to build early momentum on their team, not to penalize legitimate pre-existing plans.

Employees who change from part-time to full-time status, or vice versa, have their accrual rate recalculated prospectively from the effective date of the change; PTO already accrued under the prior schedule is not reduced.

## Accrual-Based PTO Plan

Employees below the Director level participate in the Accrual-Based PTO Plan. During years 1 through 3 of employment, employees accrue 1.25 days (10 hours) of PTO per month, totaling 15 days per year. Beginning in year 4 of continuous employment, the accrual rate increases to 1.67 days (13.33 hours) per month, totaling 20 days per year. The tenure milestone is calculated from the employee's original hire date, and the increased rate applies starting the first full month after the employee's third anniversary. Accrual is prorated for part-time employees based on their scheduled weekly hours relative to a standard 40-hour week; for example, an employee scheduled for 24 hours per week accrues 60% of the standard monthly rate.

PTO accrues monthly and is credited to the employee's balance in Compass HR on the last business day of each month, based on active employment status during that month. Accrued PTO can be viewed at any time in Compass HR under 'Time Off > My Balance,' which also shows pending requests, a running year-to-date used total, and the current carryover cap described in Section 7. Employees do not need to request a balance report from the People & Culture Team; Compass HR is the system of record for all PTO balances.

Example: an employee hired on March 15 in their second year of employment accrues 10 hours per month. By the end of a full calendar year (12 months of accrual), that employee will have accrued 120 hours, equivalent to 15 eight-hour workdays, before accounting for any PTO already used.

## Flexible PTO Plan (Director and Above)

Employees at the Director level and above participate in the Flexible PTO Plan instead of the Accrual-Based PTO Plan. Flexible PTO is not accrued and has no fixed annual maximum; time off is taken as needed subject to manager approval and business needs. Employees on the Flexible PTO Plan are strongly encouraged to take a minimum of 15 days off per calendar year to support wellbeing and avoid burnout, and managers are expected to actively encourage direct reports to use this time rather than treat 'unlimited' as an invitation to take less time off than the accrual-based plan would otherwise provide.

Because Flexible PTO is not accrued, no PTO balance is tracked in Compass HR for these employees, and no PTO payout is made upon separation from the company, since there is no accrued liability to pay out. Employees who are promoted into a Director-level role transition from the Accrual-Based Plan to the Flexible Plan effective the first day of the month following their promotion; any PTO balance accrued up to that date is paid out at the time of transition rather than carried forward indefinitely as an unused balance under a plan that no longer tracks balances.

## Requesting and Approving PTO

All PTO requests must be submitted through Compass HR under 'Time Off > Request Time Off' and approved by the employee's direct manager before the time off is taken. Verbal or chat-message approval alone does not satisfy this requirement; the request must be recorded in Compass HR so that it is reflected in the team calendar, payroll, and the employee's balance. Managers should respond to PTO requests within 2 business days of submission.

Standard advance notice windows depend on the length of the request:

- 1-2 consecutive business days: submit at least 5 business days in advance.
- 3-5 consecutive business days: submit at least 10 business days in advance.
- More than 5 consecutive business days: submit at least 30 calendar days in advance.
- Emergency or unplanned PTO (illness, family emergency) may be approved retroactively at the manager's discretion, and employees should simply notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible rather than waiting to file paperwork first.

## Minimum Notice and Blackout Periods

Employees in the Finance department may not take PTO during the last 5 business days of any fiscal quarter (the 'quarter-close blackout') except for emergencies, due to financial close obligations that require the full Finance team to be available. Solara Dynamics' fiscal year follows the calendar year, so quarter-close blackout periods fall in late March, late June, late September, and late December of each year; exact dates are published by the Finance team at the start of each quarter.

There is no company-wide blackout period outside of the Finance-specific window described above; all other departments follow only the standard notice windows in Section 5. Individual managers may temporarily restrict PTO around product launches or major customer commitments, but must communicate any such restriction to their team at least 2 weeks in advance and should avoid restricting previously-approved requests except in extraordinary circumstances.

## Carryover and Payout Rules

Employees on the Accrual-Based PTO Plan may carry over up to 5 unused PTO days into the next calendar year. Accrued PTO beyond the 5-day carryover cap is forfeited on December 31 unless applicable state law requires otherwise. To avoid losing accrued time, employees are encouraged to review their balance each November and schedule any excess days before year-end.

Employees whose primary work location is California are not subject to the forfeiture rule above: under California law, accrued and unused PTO is treated as earned wages, carries over in full without a cap, and is paid out in full upon separation, regardless of the general policy described in this section. This carve-out applies based on the employee's designated work location in Compass HR, not their state of residence if different, and is a clear example of how the Work Location, Interstate & International Work Policy (POL-LOC-001) can change which specific rules apply to an individual employee.

Upon voluntary or involuntary separation, employees on the Accrual-Based PTO Plan are paid out for unused, accrued PTO up to the plan's accrual cap described above (or in full for California-based employees). Employees on the Flexible PTO Plan receive no PTO payout upon separation, consistent with Section 4.

## Interaction with Holidays and Leave

PTO is not deducted for company-observed holidays listed in the Company Holiday Schedule Policy (POL-HOL-001) or for approved leaves of absence under the Leave of Absence Policy (POL-LEV-001). If a scheduled PTO day falls on a company holiday, the holiday takes precedence and the PTO day is credited back to the employee's balance automatically by Compass HR the following business day.

PTO taken immediately before or after an approved leave of absence (for example, using a few PTO days before parental leave begins) is permitted and is tracked separately in Compass HR so that it does not extend the length of the protected leave itself.

## Part-Time and Contractor PTO

Part-time employees accrue PTO on a prorated basis calculated as (scheduled weekly hours / 40) x the standard accrual rate for their tenure tier, as described in Section 3. For example, a part-time employee scheduled for 20 hours per week in their first year accrues 0.5 x 10 hours = 5 hours of PTO per month.

Independent contractors and EOR-employed international team members are not covered by this policy; their time-off terms are defined in their individual contractor agreement or local EOR employment contract, which may provide statutory leave entitlements that differ significantly from the plans described above. Questions about a contractor's or EOR employee's specific time-off terms should be directed to the People & Culture Team rather than assumed from this policy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take PTO during my first month? Usage is discouraged but not prohibited; if you have a pre-existing, unavoidable commitment made before your offer date, discuss it with your manager, who may approve it even though you are within your first 30 days.

What happens if my PTO request is denied? Managers may deny or ask to reschedule a request if the timing conflicts with a blackout period, insufficient notice, or a critical business need, but should provide a reason and work with the employee to find an alternative date.

Does a company holiday during my PTO count against my balance? No. As described in Section 8, if an approved PTO day falls on a company holiday listed in POL-HOL-001, that day is credited back to your balance automatically.

I am a manager - can I approve my own PTO? No. All PTO requests, including those from managers and executives, must be approved by the requester's own manager (or, for the CEO, by the Board) to keep the approval chain consistent across the company.

## Roles and Responsibilities

Employees are responsible for submitting PTO requests in Compass HR with sufficient notice, tracking their own balance, and communicating planned absences to their immediate team. Managers are responsible for reviewing and responding to requests promptly, applying blackout and notice rules consistently across their team, and ensuring adequate coverage during a direct report's absence.

The People & Culture Team owns this policy, publishes any updates to accrual rates or carryover rules, and is the point of contact for disputes about balance calculations or exceptions based on state law. Payroll is responsible for reflecting approved PTO payouts accurately in an employee's final paycheck upon separation.
